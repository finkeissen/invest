# Asset Universe

## Purpose

This file defines the initial set of assets and capital forms that connect investment decisions to future outcomes.

The universe is broader than market instruments. It should support practical reasoning for persons, groups, and geopolitical systems.

In the protection-oriented extension of `invest`, assets are not only upside-bearing positions. They are also resources, buffers, capacities, and arrangements that help preserve the conditions of a good life, reduce fragility, absorb shocks, and support recovery after loss.

## Additional classification: function of the asset

Any asset in scope may serve one or more of the following functions:
- growth or compounding
- preservation
- shock buffering
- threshold defense
- transfer of risk
- recovery support
- adaptation support

This matters because an asset that appears weak on upside may still be structurally important if it protects against irreversible or high-impact downside.

## Selection rule

Assets in scope should satisfy at least one of the following:
- they absorb shocks quickly
- they preserve future optionality
- they enable compounding
- they protect against catastrophic loss
- they proxy personal, group, or strategic resilience
- they are useful validation anchors for scenarios

## Additional review questions

When adding an asset to the universe, ask:
- what condition of life does it help preserve?
- what kind of shock can it buffer?
- what threshold does it help defend?
- does it improve real recoverability or only formal coverage?
- what maintenance burden does it create?
- can it shift fragility elsewhere while appearing protective locally?

## Important warning

Do not assume all assets are directly commensurable.

Some gains can be compared only within bounded contexts. For example, health, money, trust, and meaning should not automatically be reduced to one scalar score.

Also do not assume that nominal protection equals real safety. Some assets provide the appearance of coverage while failing under correlated stress, institutional breakdown, or delayed recovery.
