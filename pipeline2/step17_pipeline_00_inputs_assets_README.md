# Assets

This folder defines the asset universe that links investment reasoning to practical allocation.

Use this folder for financial assets, productive assets, human capital assets, and collective capital assets.

## Core idea

An asset is anything that can preserve, generate, amplify, or protect future gain.

Not all assets are market-traded.

In the extended invest frame, assets should also be classified by the conditions of life they help stabilize, the thresholds they help defend, and the forms of recovery they make possible after loss or shock.

This means that an asset is not only something that produces upside. It may also function as a buffer, a protective layer, a transfer mechanism, a recovery enabler, or an adaptive reserve.

## Typical asset families

- financial assets
- productive assets
- health assets
- educational assets
- time and attention assets
- relationship and trust assets
- institutional assets
- strategic and geopolitical assets

## Additional protective view

Each asset family should also be observed through the following questions:

- what conditions of a good life does it help preserve?
- which critical thresholds does it help defend?
- does it mainly support growth, preservation, buffering, transfer, recovery, or adaptation?
- what maintenance burden does it create?
- can it fail exactly when it is most needed?
- does it reduce fragility, or merely move fragility elsewhere?

This protective view matters because formal ownership or nominal coverage is not the same as real recoverability.
