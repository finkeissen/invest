# Constraints

This folder defines hard limits, prerequisites, and bottlenecks that shape what investments are actually executable.

Examples:
- time limits
- money limits
- health limits
- legal limits
- geographic limits
- dependency burdens
- institutional barriers
- protection gaps
- recoverability limits
- critical thresholds that must not be crossed

A practical portfolio model requires constraints to be visible early rather than added later as excuses.

This layer should also make visible which constraints are not merely inconveniences but potential pathways into severe loss, breakdown, or irreversibility.

For the purposes of life-oriented investment, constraints are not only upper bounds on action. They also define lower bounds of safety, preservation, and minimum viable functioning.

Questions to surface here include:
- Which constraints threaten the conditions of a good life if neglected?
- Which constraints are close to becoming threshold failures?
- Which constraints can be buffered, transferred, adapted to, or only narrowly endured?
- Which apparent protections are formal only and do not create real recoverability?

The presence of a constraint may therefore imply not only reduced ambition, but a need for protective investment before further expansion.
