# Priorities

This folder defines priority and sequencing logic.

Examples:
- remove catastrophic downside first
- secure basic stability before upside pursuit
- reduce headwinds before demanding heavy new commitments
- build compounding capacities after prerequisites are met
- protect critical life conditions before optional expansion
- close major protection gaps before relying on fragile upside
- preserve recoverability where losses may become irreversible

Priority rules should remain explicit rather than hidden inside later scoring logic.

In the extended direction of this repository, priorities are not only about what produces the most gain, but also about what keeps a person, household, or group above critical thresholds.

That means priority-setting should explicitly ask:
- which conditions are required for a good life to remain possible at all?
- which risks threaten those conditions directly?
- which losses are reversible, and which are not?
- what should be protected before additional expansion is pursued?
- where does nominal protection exist without real recoverability?

A practical ordering often looks like this:
1. identify critical thresholds
2. reduce or buffer catastrophic downside
3. secure minimum stability and recoverability
4. only then expand into compounding or optionality-building investments

This does not imply pure defensiveness.
It means that expansion should not quietly depend on unaddressed fragility.

Priority logic should therefore keep at least four distinctions visible:
- protection vs expansion
- near-term stability vs long-term compounding
- formal coverage vs actual recoverability
- local gain vs displaced fragility
