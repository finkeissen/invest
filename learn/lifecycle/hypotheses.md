# Lifecycle: Learner-Level Hypotheses

Non-normative description of how the framework treats claims about
a specific learner — their gaps, their interventions, their progress —
as falsifiable hypotheses rather than as findings.

---

## Why hypotheses

Within this framework, a statement of the form

> "Learner *L* has not yet reached level 3 on concept *C* because
> they lack prerequisite *P*."

is a **hypothesis**, not a finding.

It is a hypothesis because:
- it can be empirically tested by an admissible intervention,
- it can be falsified by evidence that *P* is in fact present,
- it is provisional until evaluation supports or refutes it.

Treating such statements as findings risks confirming them by acting
on them, regardless of evidence. The lifecycle below prevents that.

---

## States

A learner-level hypothesis moves through:

```
proposed  →  active  →  {supported | refuted | superseded | STOP}
```

- **proposed** — declared, not yet acted on
- **active** — at least one intervention has been declared against it
- **supported** — evaluation evidence is consistent with the hypothesis
- **refuted** — evaluation evidence is inconsistent with the hypothesis
- **superseded** — a structurally broader hypothesis has been declared
  that subsumes this one
- **STOP** — admissibility cannot be established

Refuted hypotheses are **not** deleted.
They remain in the record, with their refutation.

---

## Falsifiability obligation

A hypothesis MUST be declared with conditions under which it would
be considered refuted.

If no such conditions exist, the statement is not a hypothesis under
this framework, and is inadmissible.

This is the same falsifiability requirement that operates in
research-pipeline frameworks; it is applied here at learner scope.

---

## Why this matters operationally

A system that records every gap as a finding gradually accumulates
attributions about the learner that no evidence has tested.

A system that records every gap as a hypothesis with explicit
falsification conditions accumulates **testable claims**, and acts
on them only as long as evidence permits.

The difference is structural, not stylistic.

---

## Out of scope

- The taxonomy of typical learner-level hypotheses
- How hypotheses are surfaced to a learner or educator
- Aggregate analysis across many learners

The framework supports such activities through its contracts;
it does not specify how they are conducted.
