# 0.legacy

Earlier material retained for context.

Contents here are **not part of the framework**.
They are not normative, not authoritative, and not maintained.

Their presence implies neither endorsement nor rejection.

This directory exists so that earlier iterations remain locatable
without contaminating the canonical structure.

---

## What "legacy" means here

Legacy in this directory means **superseded within the framework's own development**:
an earlier formulation, draft, or structure that has been replaced
by a more recent one.

It does **not** mean *outdated*, *wrong*, or *no longer useful*
in any general sense.

This distinction matters.

A common confusion would be to equate "legacy" with "no longer correct".
That equation is too coarse. Consider Newtonian mechanics: it has not been
*replaced* by relativity in any meaningful sense. It remains the correct
description of motion under conditions where velocities are far below the
speed of light and gravitational fields are weak. Bridges, vehicles, and
spacecraft trajectories are computed with Newton, not with relativity.

What changed is not Newton's correctness, but the **explicit declaration
of its scope of validity**.

The framework treats such cases as concepts with **declared scope** —
not as legacy. They are current.

Legacy, in this directory, means something narrower:
material from earlier iterations of the framework's own structure
that has been replaced as the framework evolved.

---

## Why kept at all

Reading the canonical structure (`README.md`, `requirements/`, `schema/`)
gives the framework as it stands.
Reading the legacy material gives the trajectory by which it arrived.

For most readers, only the former is relevant.
For contributors, the latter occasionally explains why a decision
was made one way rather than another.

If a piece of legacy material can be lifted into the canonical
structure with full contract compliance, it ceases to be legacy.
Until then, it stays here.
